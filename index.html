Research, not hype

The cardiovascular interest has a specific research trail.

The strongest published tricaprin research focuses on triglyceride deposit cardiomyovasculopathy (TGCV) — a rare condition where abnormal triglyceride accumulation damages heart tissue. These are not general-population wellness studies. They are disease-specific. That distinction is important.

European Heart Journal 2023

Coronary atherosclerosis regression after tricaprin in TGCV patients

Researchers described two TGCV cases where dietary tricaprin was introduced after diagnosis. Both patients showed symptom improvement and regression of diffuse coronary atherosclerosis — a finding notable enough to be published in one of cardiology's flagship journals.

What the science does and doesn't show

Interesting enough to take seriously. Honest enough to say what it is.

What the research shows

Published case reports and registry studies link supplemental tricaprin to meaningful outcomes in patients with TGCV — a specific cardiovascular-metabolism disease involving abnormal triglyceride handling.

What it doesn't prove

These studies don't prove that tricaprin treats, prevents, or reverses heart disease in healthy people or the general population. The research is specific and early-stage.

That's exactly why this site stays focused on research education and cardiovascular wellness curiosity — not disease-treatment claims. Honest framing is a feature, not a disclaimer hedge.
